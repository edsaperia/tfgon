TFGON logo, colours and typeface: drafts

Logo-mark directions (round 2 first, round 1 below it for comparison), one retuned palette and two typefaces, for Ed to choose from. Each concept is shown in the retuned colours, at full size, with the network's name in both typefaces, at header and browser-tab sizes, and on a dark background. Nothing on this page is loaded from outside, and none of it is on the live site.

Files: marks in design/marks/, fonts in design/fonts/, checks in design/tools/. Brief: briefs/design-v1.md.

1. The logo mark, round 2: four new directions

Ed's review of round 1: all four marks looked like Google product logos. In words, what they shared with that family: four equal flat colours, rounded geometric shapes, arranged evenly round a centre so the mark looks the same when turned. Round 2 keeps the retuned colours and breaks that pattern on purpose. I, the pixel map, and its fully coloured versions I2 to I5 were added afterwards at Ed's request. Each direction says how. These are directions, not finished marks.

1a. Round 1, kept for comparison

All four start from the old idea, several communities coming together, without tracing the old drawing. The tab icons in the "16 px, real pixels" row are drawn at true size and then magnified, so you see exactly what a browser tab would show.

2. The colours: the old four, retuned

The same family of pink, yellow, green and blue. Pink is deepened and moved slightly towards raspberry, so it passes the text-contrast standard on white. Yellow is warmed to a golden tone so it holds its own on white. Green is softened from the old neon mint, and lifted, so pink and green stay apart for people with red–green colour blindness. Blue is a little lighter and less teal, so it sits with the others rather than looking darker than them. A near-black slate ink replaces the old grey for words, and a deep blue is the link colour.

Contrast (WCAG 2.2)

AA for normal text needs 4.5:1. Large text (24 px, or 19 px bold) and shapes such as icons and pins need 3:1.

"Against ink" means the colour as a background behind ink words, or ink on it, or the colour on the dark background. Yellow and green fail on white, even for shapes, so pins carry an ink outline that gives every pin 3:1 against the map whatever its fill. For comparison, the old colours: pink 3.62, yellow 1.44, green 1.89, blue 3.96 on white, and the old grey words 10.76.

As map pins, checked for colour blindness

Simulated with the Machado, Oliveira & Fernandes (2009) model at full strength. "Closest pair" is the CIEDE2000 colour difference between the two hardest pins to tell apart; about 10 is clearly different side by side, below about 5 they start to merge.

Old palette, same check: protanopia 13.5, deuteranopia 12.8 (pink/green), tritanopia 19.1. Under every kind of colour vision, the hardest pair to tell apart is further apart in the retuned set. The map should still never rely on colour alone: each pin will carry its community's name on hover and in its entry.

3. The typeface: two candidates

Both are open-licence geometric sans-serifs, in the friendly, round spirit of the old Strawford. They are shipped with the site's own files, regular and bold only.
