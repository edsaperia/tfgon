## The network (Ed, 2026-11-27)

- **What it is.** Its statement of purpose, agreed by all present at the May 2026 retreat in Newcastle: *"Tech for Good Organisers Network UK brings together people running tech for good communities to share knowledge, strengthen existing networks, and support new ones so the movement can have real, lasting impact."*
- **Who is in it.** Organisers of UK tech-for-good communities. In 2026–28 six member organisations each hold a £4,500 TFGON grant for local organising: Superhighways (TechSoup Connect England), ThirdSectorLab (Tech for Good Scotland), Newspeak House (London), ProMo Cymru (Tech for Good Cymru), Tech4Good South West CIC, and CAST (Digital Leads Network). North West and North East groups are emerging. The network is deliberately loose and decentralised, with no central staff. Its retro found that over-structuring hurt it, and that regular meetings hold it together better than mission statements do.
- **Its rhythm.** Four meetings a year: three online quarterlies and one in-person retreat. The 2027 retreat is in Bristol, organised by Annie Legge. The next quarterly is on 3 March 2027.
- **Why a website now.** At the 25 Nov 2026 quarterly, the most-voted topic was the network's public profile (Annie: *"I often get asked about other groups to connect to outside the South West"*). Notes from that discussion: website, sign-up, the closest member does the onboarding, regular events and an annual retreat, joining mostly by invitation, a newsletter. Ed took the action to put this together.
- **Background source.** Ed's Google Doc *TFGON 2026 Onwards*: members, the 2026–28 programme, retreat and meeting notes. Not all of it is for the website, but it informs the site's structure.

## The website

- **The existing site** is https://www.techforgoodorganisers.uk/. It was inherited and has not been updated in a long time. Its credentials are lost, but the network controls the domain, so a new site can be put on it. Its assets may be reused, but a redesign is expected (Ed, 2026-11-27). The coordinator reviewed it on 2026-11-27, after Ed allowed access to it and to Webflow's file server. It is a Webflow site, copyright 2023. It says the network covers the UK and Ireland, has founding members from ten communities (several since lapsed), meets monthly, holds two retreats a year, uses Slack and Notion, and was funded through Catalyst. Its join route was an email to a network coordinator at hello@techforgoodorganisers.uk, an inbox nobody now monitors (planning doc). Its "network map" was an outside Kumu map. The privacy and code-of-conduct pages say only "coming soon". The logo, its colours and the homepage text are saved in `reference/old-site/`, which Pages does not publish.
- **Old-site assets: each is decided one at a time and refreshed, never reused as it was** (Ed, 2026-11-27). The list: the logo, its colours, the typeface, the definition of tech for good, the principles, and the founding-members list.
  - **Logo: a new mark built on the old idea, not the old drawing** (Ed, 2026-11-27): several communities coming together, perhaps tied to the directory's UK map. It is a real design job, and Ed chooses from drafts. The old mark (four overlapping strokes) is in `reference/old-site/`.
  - **Colours: the same four-colour family, retuned** (Ed, 2026-11-27): pink, yellow, green and blue, adjusted so they sit well together. The bright tones are used for shapes (the mark, map pins, highlights), and a dark ink is used for words and links, so all text passes the accessibility contrast standard. The old values were pink `#EF476F`, yellow `#FFD166`, green `#06D6A0`, blue `#118AB2` and words `#393E46`.
  - **Typeface: one open-licence typeface in the old site's geometric, friendly spirit, shipped with the site's own files** (Ed, 2026-11-27). Nothing is loaded from a font service. The designer proposes two candidates (for example Figtree or Outfit) alongside the logo drafts, and Ed picks. The same typeface sets the network's name beside the new mark. The old site used Strawford and Sentient, both commercial.
  - **Definition of tech for good: only the one-line definition, in the intro** (Ed, 2026-11-27): *"the intentional use of technology to drive positive outcomes for people and planet"* (the old site's words, possibly lightly reworded at copy review). The site does not define what a community is.
  - **Principles: dropped** (Ed, 2026-11-27). The site has no principles or "how we work" list. The statement of purpose carries the network's values.
  - **Past members: a folded list at the foot of the directory** (Ed, 2026-11-27), framed as communities that helped build the network. Each entry has the community's name, its region, rough years where known, a link if its site still exists, and its founding organisers' names (Ed: keeping the names is fine). Past communities are **not** on the map, which shows the network's reach today. A community that restarts moves back into the directory. Draft list from the old site's founding members, which Ed must confirm as past: Tech for Good London / Global, Tech for Good Live (Manchester), Tech for Good Oxford, Digital for Good (Scotland), Tech for Good Exeter, Social Tech North East, and NetSquared Midlands.
- **Who the site is for:** **organisers who might join the network** and **funders and partners** (Ed, 2026-11-27). It is not mainly for the general public looking for a local group. So the directory shows who is in the network and where they are, and "reach out" is for prospective members and potential funders or partners to contact organisers.
- **Who keeps it up to date:** **Ed, through the coordinator, for now** (Ed, 2026-11-27). Every page, event and directory entry is a plain file in this repository. Members send Ed their changes, and the coordinator makes them. Opening up editing to members later must not need the site rebuilt.
- **The site is one page with folding sections**, not several pages, as Ed prefers (Ed, 2026-11-27). The sections, in order: **intro**, **TFGON events**, **join**, **directory**.
- **The map is a drawn map of the UK, built into the page**, with one pin per community. It needs no outside map service, and clicking a pin unfolds that community's entry (Ed, 2026-11-27).
- **What the site must have:** a **directory of organisers with a map**, and **a way to reach out** to them. Perhaps more, to be designed (Ed, 2026-11-27).
- **The directory: one entry per community** (Ed, 2026-11-27). Each **community** has its name, region and map pin, a line on what it does, its website and events link, and the **organisers** who run it, named inside the entry. An organiser's photo and contact details appear only with that person's consent. When an organiser moves on, their name changes and the community stays on the map.
- **Reaching out** (Ed, 2026-11-27). Organisers' email addresses in the directory are public, each with that organiser's consent, so anyone can contact a community directly. **Applications** to join take a separate route. The applicant fills in a short form: their name, an email address to reply to, and links to tech-for-good events they have organised. The form has no location field, because "nearest" is judged from where those events were held. The network looks at the events, forwards the application to the nearest organiser or organisers, and adds it to the agenda of the next meeting. The network already has an email address for each organiser.
- **Where applications land** (Ed, 2026-11-27, replacing the Google Form chosen earlier that day). The site's own form sends each application to a **DigitalOcean Function**, a few lines of code on Ed's existing DigitalOcean account, where Topic runs. The function holds the GitHub key and opens a GitHub **issue** for the application. Claude then reviews the issue on GitHub Actions: are the events real and tech-for-good, where were they held, which community and organiser is nearest, plus a draft forwarding note. People still send the forwarding email and make the decision. This keeps the services to GitHub, DigitalOcean and Claude, with no Google Form (Ed wants as few services as possible). If sending fails, the form tells the applicant and shows a fallback email address. Applications live in a **second, private repository** (planned name `edsaperia/tfgon-applications`). Ed chose two repositories over making this one private (Ed, 2026-11-27). This repository stays public and holds only the site.

## Glossary

Names for the parts of TFGON, literal and stable, added as they are decided.

- **TFGON**: the Tech for Good Organisers Network.
- **Community**: one tech-for-good group in the network (for example Tech for Good Scotland), shown as one entry and one pin in the directory.
- **Organiser**: a person who runs a community, named within that community's entry.
- **Directory**: the site's list of communities, together with its map.
- **Application**: a would-be member organiser's request to join, made through the site's application form. It is reviewed, forwarded to the nearest organiser, and added to the next meeting's agenda.
