## The network (Ed, 2026-11-27)

- **What it is.** Its statement of purpose, agreed by all present at the May 2026 retreat in Newcastle: *"Tech for Good Organisers Network UK brings together people running tech for good communities to share knowledge, strengthen existing networks, and support new ones so the movement can have real, lasting impact."*
- **Who is in it.** Organisers of UK tech-for-good communities. In 2026–28 six member organisations each hold a £4,500 TFGON grant for local organising: Superhighways (TechSoup Connect England), ThirdSectorLab (Tech for Good Scotland), Newspeak House (London), ProMo Cymru (Tech for Good Cymru), Tech4Good South West CIC, and CAST (Digital Leads Network). North West and North East groups are emerging. The network is deliberately loose and decentralised, with no central staff. Its retro found that over-structuring hurt it, and that regular meetings hold it together better than mission statements do.
- **Its rhythm.** Four meetings a year: three online quarterlies and one in-person retreat. The 2027 retreat is in Bristol, organised by Annie Legge. The next quarterly is on 3 March 2027.
- **Why a website now.** At the 25 Nov 2026 quarterly, the most-voted topic was the network's public profile (Annie: *"I often get asked about other groups to connect to outside the South West"*). Notes from that discussion: website, sign-up, the closest member does the onboarding, regular events and an annual retreat, joining mostly by invitation, a newsletter. Ed took the action to put this together.
- **Background source.** Ed's Google Doc *TFGON 2026 Onwards*: members, the 2026–28 programme, retreat and meeting notes. Not all of it is for the website, but it informs the site's structure.

## The website

- **The existing site** is https://www.techforgoodorganisers.uk/. It was inherited and has not been updated in a long time. Its credentials are lost, but the network controls the domain, so a new site can be put on it. Its assets may be reused, but a redesign is expected (Ed, 2026-11-27). The old site and web.archive.org are both blocked from the coordinator's cloud container, so the coordinator has not seen it yet. Its old *join* form probably went to an inbox nobody monitored (planning doc).
- **Who the site is for:** **organisers who might join the network** and **funders and partners** (Ed, 2026-11-27). It is not mainly for the general public looking for a local group. So the directory shows who is in the network and where they are, and "reach out" is for prospective members and potential funders or partners to contact organisers.
- **Who keeps it up to date:** **Ed, through the coordinator, for now** (Ed, 2026-11-27). Every page, event and directory entry is a plain file in this repository. Members send Ed their changes, and the coordinator makes them. Opening up editing to members later must not need the site rebuilt.
- **What the site must have:** a **directory of organisers with a map**, and **a way to reach out** to them. Perhaps more, to be designed (Ed, 2026-11-27).
- **The directory: one entry per community** (Ed, 2026-11-27). Each **community** has its name, region and map pin, a line on what it does, its website and events link, and the **organisers** who run it, named inside the entry. An organiser's photo and contact details appear only with that person's consent. When an organiser moves on, their name changes and the community stays on the map.
- **Reaching out** (Ed, 2026-11-27). Organisers' email addresses in the directory are public, each with that organiser's consent, so anyone can contact a community directly. **Applications** to join take a separate route. The applicant fills in a short form: their name, an email address to reply to, and links to tech-for-good events they have organised. The form has no location field, because "nearest" is judged from where those events were held. The network looks at the events, forwards the application to the nearest organiser or organisers, and adds it to the agenda of the next meeting. The network already has an email address for each organiser.
- **Where applications land** (Ed, 2026-11-27, replacing the Google Form chosen earlier that day). The site's own form sends each application to a **DigitalOcean Function**, a few lines of code on Ed's existing DigitalOcean account, where Topic runs. The function holds the GitHub key and opens a GitHub **issue** for the application. Claude then reviews the issue on GitHub Actions: are the events real and tech-for-good, where were they held, which community and organiser is nearest, plus a draft forwarding note. People still send the forwarding email and make the decision. This keeps the services to GitHub, DigitalOcean and Claude, with no Google Form (Ed wants as few services as possible). If sending fails, the form tells the applicant and shows a fallback email address. Ed wants **one repository, not two**, so whether this repository becomes private is being decided.

## Glossary

Names for the parts of TFGON, literal and stable, added as they are decided.

- **TFGON**: the Tech for Good Organisers Network.
- **Community**: one tech-for-good group in the network (for example Tech for Good Scotland), shown as one entry and one pin in the directory.
- **Organiser**: a person who runs a community, named within that community's entry.
- **Directory**: the site's list of communities, together with its map.
- **Application**: a would-be member organiser's request to join, made through the site's application form. It is reviewed, forwarded to the nearest organiser, and added to the next meeting's agenda.
